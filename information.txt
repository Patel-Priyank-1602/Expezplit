1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORE VALUE PROPOSITION
================================================================================
Expezplit is an all-in-one financial operating system that unifies personal 
budget tracking, intelligent multi-currency conversion, real-time debt 
simplification, and peer-to-peer settlement orchestration into a single, cohesive, 
zero-latency web and mobile application.

The Core Problem Matrix:
1. Fragmented Personal vs. Group Finances:
   Consumers traditionally manage personal budgets in one app (e.g., Mint, 
   YNAB, or Excel) while managing shared group expenses in another (e.g., 
   Splitwise). This creates data silos and double-entry fatigue.
2. The "N*(N-1) Who Owes Whom" Payment Chaos:
   In group outings (travel, shared housing, dinners), multiple people pay for 
   different items. Standard naive splitting results in an unmanageable web of 
   reciprocal payments, confusion, and awkward social friction.
3. Multi-Currency Travel Nightmares:
   When traveling internationally, group members spend in foreign currencies 
   (EUR, USD, GBP, JPY, AED, etc.). Computing manual exchange rates at the end 
   of a trip is tedious, prone to mathematical drift, and causes disputes.
4. Settlement Friction & Manual Data Entry:
   Copy-pasting bank account numbers, phone numbers, or searching for handles 
   across multiple payment apps introduces severe friction.
5. Ineffective Manual Reminders:
   Chasing friends or housemates for unpaid debts is socially uncomfortable.

The Expezplit Solution:
- Dual-Engine Cohesion: Seamlessly switch between personal expense analytics 
  and collaborative group bill splitting under a single authenticated account.
- Algorithmic Debt Minimization: Employs a Greedy Graph Cash Flow Minimization 
  Engine that collapses up to N*(N-1)/2 complex debts into at most N-1 direct 
  transfers.
- Automated Real-Time FX Engine: Normalizes all global transactions into a base 
  canonical currency (INR) in PostgreSQL, while fetching live foreign exchange 
  rates from an external API every 5 minutes to support on-the-fly currency 
  switching across 160+ fiat currencies.
- One-Tap Hardware & Deep-Link Settlement: Native web camera QR code scanning 
  (html5-qrcode), instant invite/payment QR generation (qrcode.react), and 
  zero-click UPI deep links (upi://pay) that directly launch mobile payment apps.
- Autonomous Email Dispatch Microservice: A decoupled Node.js Express server 
  utilizing Nodemailer to automatically dispatch structured HTML expense 
  breakdowns and settlement reminders with payer UPI information.
- 100% Free & Open Architecture: Zero paywalls, unlimited groups, unlimited 
  currencies, and complete CSV export capabilities.

2. TARGET AUDIENCE, PERSONAS & REAL-WORLD USE CASES
================================================================================
Expezplit was engineered to cater to both individual financial planners and 
high-frequency collaborative spending groups:

Persona 1: Roommates & Flatmates
- Real-World Scenario: 3 to 6 flatmates sharing apartment rent, electricity 
  bills, WiFi, maid charges, groceries, and cleaning supplies.
- Key Pain Points: Recurring monthly expenses, unequal split shares (e.g., one 
  roommate had guests over or occupies a larger master bedroom), forgetting who 
  paid what.
- Expezplit Solution: Persistent group rooms, custom itemized splits, automated 
  monthly balance aggregation, and instant UPI QR payments.

Persona 2: International Backpackers & Group Travelers
- Real-World Scenario: A group of 4 friends traveling across Europe and Asia, 
  incurring expenses in EUR, GBP, CHF, and JPY.
- Key Pain Points: Constantly fluctuating foreign exchange rates, conversion math 
  errors, members paying in local cash while others use cards.
- Expezplit Solution: Live Multi-Currency Conversion Engine that normalizes all 
  foreign purchases into base currency, showing real-time balance conversions.

Persona 3: Friend Circles & Event Hosts
- Real-World Scenario: Group dinners, concert outings, weekend road trips, 
  birthday gifts, and festival celebrations.
- Key Pain Points: Onboarding 10+ people quickly without making them download 
  heavy apps or create complex accounts; chasing people for minor splits.
- Expezplit Solution: Instant 9-character invite codes, QR camera scanning via 
  Google Lens or browser camera, and automated Nodemailer email notifications.

Persona 4: Solo Budgeters & Personal Finance Enthusiasts
- Real-World Scenario: Tracking daily spending on food, transit, shopping, bills, 
  and leisure to maintain a monthly savings rate.
- Key Pain Points: Lack of visual insights, tedious logging interfaces.
- Expezplit Solution: Personal Expense Tracker with category tagging, Recharts 
  interactive visual analytics (Area, Bar, Pie, RadialBar), spending run-rate 
  predictions, and RFC 4180 CSV export.

Persona 5: Freelance Collaborators & Project Teams
- Real-World Scenario: Small agency teams incurring shared software licenses, 
  cloud hosting costs, and project travel expenses.
- Key Pain Points: Need structured audit trails, transaction records, and clean 
  exports for tax/accounting purposes.
- Expezplit Solution: Comprehensive Splitwise transaction exports and personal 
  expense exports with timestamped CSV files.

4.2 AUTHENTICATION & USER PROFILE LIFECYCLE PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Initial Load: On app launch, `main.tsx` mounts `<ClerkProvider>` using 
   `VITE_CLERK_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`.
2. Session Evaluation: `App.tsx` calls `useUser()`. If `!isLoaded`, a shimmer 
   skeleton screen is displayed.
3. Unauthenticated State: If `!isSignedIn`, the application renders `HomePage.tsx` 
   (interactive landing page with hero demo simulator, live sandbox calculator, 
   comparison table, and modal login triggers).
4. Authenticated State: If `isSignedIn`, the user is admitted to the main dashboard 
   shell.
5. Profile Avatar Synchronization: In `Splitwise.tsx`, an automated `useEffect` hook 
   checks if the user's `avatar_url` is stored in `group_members`. If null, it 
   synchronizes Clerk's `user.imageUrl` directly into the database.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 DATABASE QUERY, RLS & MUTATION PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Client Query: React components call Supabase client SDK methods (`.from('groups').select('*')`).
2. Authentication Headers: Supabase client sends standard HTTPS requests with the 
   anonymous public key and user identifier.
3. Row Level Security: PostgreSQL evaluates table-level RLS policies (`using (true)` 
   or matching `user_id`).
4. Relational Data Assembly: In `Splitwise.tsx`, `fetchGroups` performs parallel queries 
   for groups owned by the user (`user_id = userId`) and groups joined via invite code 
   (`group_members.email = userEmail`). It then batch-fetches corresponding members, 
   expenses, and splits, assembling them in memory into structured Group objects.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 REAL-TIME WEBSOCKETS NOTIFICATION PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Expense Created: Adding a group expense calls `createExpenseNotifications()`.
2. Supabase Insert: Generates two sets of notifications in the `notifications` table:
   - Type 'pay': For each owing member (amount = member's split amount).
   - Type 'receive': For the payer (amount = 0, `split_members` jsonb contains list 
     of owing members and settlement statuses).
3. Realtime Broadcast: Because `supabase_realtime` publication includes `notifications`, 
   PostgreSQL emits a WebSocket event.
4. Client Receiver: `Notifications.tsx` listens on channel `notifications-realtime` 
   for `postgres_changes`. On payload arrival, it updates the unread badge count and 
   refreshes the notification drawer in real time.
5. Instant In-App Settle: Clicking "Settle" inside the notification executes 
   `settleNotification()`, updates DB records, and dispatches a custom DOM event 
   `expense-settled`, prompting all active group balance views to re-fetch and 
   update instantly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5 ASYNCHRONOUS TRANSACTIONAL EMAIL MICROSERVICE PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Trigger: When an expense is submitted in `Splitwise.tsx`, 
   `sendExpenseEmailNotifications()` is fired asynchronously (non-blocking).
2. HTTP Dispatch: Client sends a `POST` request to `http://localhost:3001/api/send-email` 
   (or configured `VITE_EMAIL_API_URL`).
3. Payload Validation: `backend/emailServer.mjs` validates required fields 
   (`participants`, `groupName`, `payerName`, `payerEmail`, `expenseDescription`).
4. Payer Filtering: The backend automatically filters out the payer's email from 
   the recipient list so they do not receive redundant emails.
5. SMTP Execution: Nodemailer initializes a transport over `smtp.gmail.com:587` with 
   `dns.setDefaultResultOrder("ipv4first")` to prevent IPv6 DNS timeout errors.
6. HTML Template Rendering: Generates a responsive HTML email with an itemized 
   breakdown table highlighting the group name, who paid, amount to pay, and payer UPI 
   ID.
7. Result Aggregation: Returns `{ success: true, sent: count, results: [...] }` to the 
   frontend.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.6 LIVE MULTI-CURRENCY CONVERSION & POLLING PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Canonical Base: All amounts in PostgreSQL (`expenses`, `group_expenses`, 
   `group_expense_splits`) are persisted strictly in Base Currency (`INR`).
2. Rate Fetching: On component mount, the client queries:
   `https://v6.exchangerate-api.com/v6/{KEY}/latest/INR`
3. AbortController: An AbortController ensures clean cleanup if components unmount 
   during in-flight HTTP requests.
4. Auto-Refresh Interval: Rates are automatically re-polled every 5 minutes 
   (`RATE_REFRESH_MS = 300000`).
5. Real-Time Conversion Formulas:
   - Base to Display: Amount_display = Amount_base * Conversion_rate
   - Display to Base: Amount_base = Amount_display / Conversion_rate
6. Fallback Chain: If API fails or is offline:
   Live API -> In-Memory State -> LocalStorage -> Static Base Rate (1.0).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.7 QR CODE GENERATION, SCANNING & DEEP-LINKED SETTLEMENT PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Group Creation: Generates a cryptographically shuffled 9-character code 
   (3 digits + 3 letters + 3 symbols, e.g., `7x#9kP$2m`).
2. Join Link Encoding: Client generates URL: `https://<domain>/?join=7x#9kP$2m`.
3. QR Code Display: Rendered via `<QRCodeSVG>` inside the Group Share modal.
4. Scanning In-App: `html5-qrcode` initiates camera stream. Upon decoding a QR code, 
   it extracts the `join` query parameter or raw code and auto-fills the join input.
5. Scanning via External Camera / Google Lens: User opening the URL directly is routed 
   to the app; `App.tsx` detects `?join=` in `window.location.search`, automatically 
   switches active tab to `splitwise`, and auto-fills the join modal.
6. UPI Settlement Flow:
   - Payee registers `upi_id` (e.g., `user@okaxis` or `payee@upi`).
   - Debtor clicks "Pay via UPI" on a simplified debt card.
   - App renders dynamic UPI QR code (`upi://pay?pa=...&pn=...&am=...&cu=INR`).
   - Debtor clicks "Launch UPI App" -> browser triggers deep link `upi://pay`, 
     seamlessly opening Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, or BHIM with amount and payee 
     pre-filled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.8 RFC 4180 DATA EXPORT PIPELINE (PERSONAL & GROUP CSVS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Trigger: User clicks the "CSV" export button in the navigation header.
2. Parallel Extraction: `handleDownloadAllCsv()` invokes `downloadExpensesCsv()` and 
   `downloadSplitwiseCsv()` concurrently.
3. Personal Expenses CSV:
   - Fetches all expenses for `user_id`.
   - Formats fields: `date`, `name`, `category`, `amount`, `currency`.
4. Group Transactions CSV:
   - Fetches all owned groups and joined groups.
   - Queries `group_members`, `group_expenses`, and `group_expense_splits`.
   - Distinguishes standard expenses from settlements (`type = "settlement"` if 
     description is "Payment").
   - Maps columns: `group`, `type`, `description`, `total_amount`, `paid_by`, 
     `split_with`, `split_amount`, `split_type`, `currency`, `date`.
5. RFC 4180 Escaping: Special characters (commas, quotes, newlines) are properly 
   escaped (`replace(/"/g, '""')`).
6. Blob Trigger: Creates an in-memory `Blob([csvContent], { type: "text/csv;charset=utf-8;" })`, 
   attaches a temporary `<a>` element, clicks it programmatically, and revokes the 
   Object URL.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 DETAILED TABLE DDL & RELATIONAL SCHEMA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. TABLE: `expenses` (Personal Expense Tracker)
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Column Name | Data Type      | Constraints & Defaults
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   id          | uuid           | PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid()
   user_id     | text           | NOT NULL
   name        | text           | NOT NULL
   category    | text           | NOT NULL
   amount      | numeric(12,2)  | NOT NULL CHECK (amount > 0)
   currency    | text           | NOT NULL DEFAULT '₹'
   created_at  | timestamptz    | NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. TABLE: `groups` (Splitwise Groups)
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Column Name   | Data Type     | Constraints & Defaults
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   id            | uuid          | PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid()
   user_id       | text          | NOT NULL (Group Creator Clerk ID)
   name          | text          | NOT NULL
   currency      | text          | NOT NULL DEFAULT '₹'
   admin_user_id | text          | NULLABLE (Admin Clerk User ID)
   invite_code   | text          | UNIQUE NULLABLE (9-character join code)
   created_at    | timestamptz   | NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. TABLE: `group_members` (Group Roster & Payment Metadata)
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Column Name     | Data Type     | Constraints & Defaults
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   id              | uuid          | PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid()
   group_id        | uuid          | NOT NULL REFERENCES groups(id) ON DELETE CASCADE
   name            | text          | NOT NULL
   email           | text          | NOT NULL
   is_current_user | boolean       | NOT NULL DEFAULT false
   avatar_url      | text          | NULLABLE (User profile picture URL)
   upi_id          | text          | NULLABLE (Payee UPI VPA handle)
   created_at      | timestamptz   | NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. TABLE: `group_expenses` (Group Transaction Entries)
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Column Name | Data Type      | Constraints & Defaults
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   id          | uuid           | PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid()
   group_id    | uuid           | NOT NULL REFERENCES groups(id) ON DELETE CASCADE
   description | text           | NOT NULL
   amount      | numeric(12,2)  | NOT NULL CHECK (amount > 0)
   paid_by_id  | uuid           | NOT NULL REFERENCES group_members(id) ON DELETE CASCADE
   split_type  | text           | NOT NULL CHECK (split_type IN ('equal', 'custom'))
   created_at  | timestamptz    | NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. TABLE: `group_expense_splits` (Member Split Breakdown)
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Column Name | Data Type      | Constraints & Defaults

5.2 FOREIGN KEYS, CASCADES & INTEGRITY CONSTRAINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Cascading Deletes: Deleting a group in `groups` automatically cascades and purges 
  all associated records in `group_members`, `group_expenses`, `group_expense_splits`, 
  and `notifications`.
- Expense Cascade: Deleting an individual expense in `group_expenses` automatically 
  cascades and deletes its child rows in `group_expense_splits` and `notifications`.
- Member Constraint: If a member in `group_members` is referenced in `group_expenses` 
  or `group_expense_splits`, deletion is blocked by foreign key constraints until 
  related expenses are removed, preventing dangling financial balances.

6. MATHEMATICAL MODELS & ALGORITHMIC ENGINES

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 GREEDY MINIMUM CASH FLOW GRAPH SIMPLIFICATION ALGORITHM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Mathematical Challenge:
In a group of N members with M expenses, multiple members pay for varying shares. 
A naive ledger settlement requires every debtor to settle directly with every payer 
they owe. This creates an unmanageable complete directed graph with up to 
N*(N-1)/2 transactions.

The Expezplit Greedy Optimization Solution:
Expezplit treats debt settlement as a Maximum Bipartite Cash Flow Minimization 
problem, reducing the total transaction volume to at most N-1 transactions.

Algorithmic Steps:
1. Net Balance Computation:
   For every member i in {1, 2, ..., N}, calculate their net balance B_i:
   B_i = SUM(Amount Paid by i) - SUM(Amount Owed by i)
   
   Fundamental Conservation Law:
   The algebraic sum of all net balances in a closed group is strictly zero:
   SUM(B_i) = 0 for all i in {1, ..., N}

2. Disjoint Partitioning:
   Filter and partition members into two distinct priority arrays:
   - Debtors Set  D = { (i, |B_i|) | B_i < -0.01 }
   - Creditors Set C = { (j, B_j)  | B_j >  0.01 }
   (An epsilon guard of 0.01 prevents floating-point rounding noise from creating 
   micro-transactions).

3. Greedy Sorting & Iterative Matching:
   - Sort D in descending order of debt magnitude.
   - Sort C in descending order of credit magnitude.
   - Initialize pointer i = 0 (Debtors) and pointer j = 0 (Creditors).
   - While i < |D| and j < |C|:
     * Let current debtor d = D[i] and current creditor c = C[j].
     * Settle Amount S = min(debt_d, credit_c).
     * Output optimized directed transfer: (d -> S -> c).
     * Update remaining balances:
       debt_d   = debt_d - S
       credit_c = credit_c - S
     * If debt_d < 0.01: advance debtor pointer i = i + 1.
     * If credit_c < 0.01: advance creditor pointer j = j + 1.

Complexity & Efficiency Analysis:
- Naive Approach Transactions : O(N^2)
- Expezplit Greedy Transactions: At most N - 1 transfers (O(N))
- Time Complexity              : O(N log N) dominated by sorting
- Space Complexity             : O(N) auxiliary memory for balance vectors

Numerical Example:
Group Members: Alice, Bob, Charlie, David
- Alice paid $120 for Dinner (split equally $30 each).
- Bob paid $60 for Taxi (split equally $15 each).
- Net Balances:
  * Alice  : +$120 - $45 = +$75 (Creditor)
  * Bob    : +$60  - $45 = +$15 (Creditor)
  * Charlie: -$45  (Debtor)
  * David  : -$45  (Debtor)
- Greedy Matching Output:
  1. David owes Alice $45. (Alice remaining credit: $30, David settled).
  2. Charlie owes Alice $30. (Alice settled, Charlie remaining debt: $15).
  3. Charlie owes Bob $15. (Both settled).
- Total Transactions: 3 transfers instead of 6 naive transfers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2 LIVE MULTI-CURRENCY CONVERSION ENGINE & PRECISION ARITHMETIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To maintain absolute database integrity while providing global UX versatility:
1. Canonical Base Principle:
   All database records store amounts normalized in Base Currency (`INR`).
2. Forward Transformation (Rendering to User Display Currency T):
   Amount_display = Amount_base * Rate_target
3. Inverse Transformation (Saving User Input in Currency T to Database):
   Amount_base = Amount_display / Rate_target
4. Precision Preservation:
   - All conversions compute using 64-bit IEEE 754 floating-point numbers.
   - Formatting rounds to 2 decimal places (`.toFixed(2)`) strictly during UI 
     presentation, preventing cumulative truncation loss.
5. Lifecycle & Network Management:
   - Polling interval set to 300,000 ms (5 minutes).
   - Component unmounting triggers `AbortController.abort()` to cancel pending fetches.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3 FINANCIAL ANALYTICS, VELOCITY & RUN-RATE PROJECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Inside `Analytics.tsx`, multiple mathematical models power real-time financial 
projections:

1. Category Distribution Share:
   Share_cat = (SUM(Expenses in Category) / SUM(Total Expenses)) * 100%

2. Daily Spending Velocity:
   Velocity_daily = Total Spent in Period / Number of Days in Period

3. Monthly Run-Rate Projection (Predictive Burn Rate):
   Let S_curr be total spending in current month up to day D_curr, and D_total be 
   the total days in the month:
   Projected_Month_End_Expense = (S_curr / D_curr) * D_total

4. Month-over-Month Spending Acceleration:
   MoM_Delta = ((Spent_Current_Month - Spent_Previous_Month) / Spent_Previous_Month) * 100%

5. Spending Streaks & Peak Day Identification:
   Iterates through calendar intervals to identify contiguous spending days and 
   the maximum single-day outlay.

================================================================================
1. Authentication Security:
   - Managed entirely through Clerk's SOC-2 compliant identity platform.
   - Passwords and OAuth tokens are never touched or stored by Expezplit servers.
   - Session tokens (JWTs) are transmitted over TLS with short expiry lifetimes.

2. Database Security:
   - PostgreSQL Row Level Security (RLS) policies enforce authorization.
   - Foreign key integrity constraints with cascading deletes prevent orphan records.
   - Zero storage of sensitive banking credentials (no bank account numbers, PINs, 
     or card details; only public UPI VPA handles).

3. Communication Security:
   - All API endpoints communicate strictly over HTTPS.
   - Nodemailer SMTP connection operates over TLS Port 587 with Gmail App Password authentication.
   - Sensitive environment variables are kept in `.env` and `.env.local`, excluded 
     from Git version control via `.gitignore`.
